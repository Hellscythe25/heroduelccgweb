## 1. Who is responsible for your data

The controller of your data is **Wildcat Games**, the game's developer: it runs the server and handles requests about your data. Inca Games is the game's publisher. [PENDING: Wildcat Games' legal name and address, if the legal review requires them.]

For any question or request about your data, write to **{{privacyContact}}**.

## 2. What we keep

- **Your account, if you create one:** your username and your password. The password is stored transformed so that no one can read it, not even us. The account is created on a server page that opens in your browser; you never type your password inside the game.
- **Your game installation:** an identifier and a key that the game itself generates to recognize your device. On your device, the game keeps that key protected, along with your player identifier, which works as your support ID.
- **The game version:** on every connection, the game sends its version and protocol number, so it can tell you when you need to update.
- **Your invitation code:** it is used once to join the beta; the game does not keep it on your device.
- **Your profile:** your public name (with its number), your friends, your friend requests and the players you block.
- **Your progress:** your cards, your decks, your in-game coins, your scores, your level and the matches you play.
- **The days you open the game:** only the date, not the time, so we can send gifts to players who played on certain days (for example, compensation if the server fails). They are kept for 90 days and then deleted automatically.
- **Your gifts:** the mailbox gifts you have yet to claim and the ones you already claimed, with their date, so no one receives the same gift twice.
- **Usage events:** notes on what happens in the game, such as the matches you start and finish, to learn how the beta is working. They only carry identifiers and counters from a closed list; they carry no data about your device.
- **Technical logs:** if a connection makes too many attempts in a row, the server records that connection's internet address to protect itself from abuse.

We do not ask for your real name, your email, your phone number or any payment details. **There are no real-money purchases in this beta.**

## 3. What other players see

Your opponent sees your public name, your hero, your avatar and your frame, and your name stays in their match history. Your friends see your name and whether you are online. The leaderboard shows public names and scores.

That is why we ask you not to include your real name or other personal data in your public name.

## 4. What we use it for

Only so you can play: signing in to your account, keeping your progress, playing with your friends and keeping the service running and protected. We do not sell your data or use it for advertising.

## 5. Who else is involved

When you connect over the internet, the connection goes through **Cloudflare**'s network, which protects it. Cloudflare sees your internet address and can see what travels between your device and the server, including what you type on the account pages. The account pages also load its "Turnstile" check, which verifies that a person is typing. Cloudflare handles that data under its own [privacy policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/). No one else receives your data.

## 7. How to delete your account and your data

Write to **{{privacyContact}}** with your username. If you play without an account, give your full public name, with its number (or your support ID, which you can find in Options). We may ask you to confirm that the account is yours.

We delete your account, the record of your devices, your public name, your friends, your progress, your coins, your scores, your usage events, the days you opened the game, and the gifts you received and had yet to claim. Deletion cannot be undone.

After deletion, the following remains:

- the internal log of administrative actions (for example, a coin gift, a block or the deletion itself), which uses an internal identifier, not your name;
- the matches you played with other people, which still show that identifier but no longer the name you played under;
- earlier backups, until they expire.
